In-House Cleaning Staff vs. a Commercial Cleaning Company: The Real Cost Comparison

What an in-house cleaner actually costs once you count taxes, turnover, supplies, and coverage gaps.

Blog

On the surface, hiring your own cleaning staff looks like the cheaper option — one wage, direct control over the schedule. But the full cost picture usually tells a different story once you count everything involved.

The Visible Cost

Most businesses calculate in-house cleaning cost as hourly wage times hours worked. That number misses a significant share of the real expense.

Costs Most Businesses Overlook

Payroll taxes and benefits. Employer-side taxes and any benefits typically add a meaningful percentage on top of base wages.

Recruiting and onboarding. Hiring and training a new cleaning employee takes real time and cost when a position turns over.

Turnover. Cleaning staff positions turn over more often than most roles. Every departure means retraining and a gap in coverage.

Supplies and equipment. Commercial vacuums, floor machines, and professional-grade chemicals require upfront purchase and ongoing maintenance.

Supervision time. Someone on your team manages the cleaner's schedule and performance — real management overhead that's rarely counted.

Liability exposure. If an in-house cleaner is injured on the job, that's a workers' comp claim against your policy. A commercial cleaning company carries its own coverage.

Coverage gaps. When your in-house cleaner is out sick or resigns, you're left scrambling. A commercial cleaning company provides backup coverage as part of the contract.

When In-House Makes Sense

For very large facilities with full-time, dedicated cleaning needs, an in-house team can make sense. For most small and mid-sized Saint Paul businesses, outsourcing delivers more consistent coverage without the overhead.
