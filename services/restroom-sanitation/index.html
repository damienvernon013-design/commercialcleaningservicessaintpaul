Fixture disinfection, supply management, odor control, and written logs. For buildings from 10 occupants to 300.

Restroom Sanitation

Restrooms are the most-inspected space in any commercial building and the fastest to deteriorate without a consistent program. A missed night in a high-traffic commercial restroom is visible by the next morning. A missed week in a low-occupancy suite produces odor and buildup that takes two or three services to reverse.

Our restroom sanitation programs for Saint Paul facilities are scaled to occupancy — the scope for a 15-person office is different from the scope for a 200-person medical building, and both are different from a retail common-area restroom with variable daily traffic.

What the Program Covers

  • Toilet and urinal disinfection, inside and outside
  • Sink and countertop disinfection
  • Mirror cleaning
  • Partition wipe-down
  • Floor mopping with disinfectant solution
  • Trash removal and liner replacement
  • Soap, paper towel, and toilet paper restocking
  • Drain maintenance (monthly or as needed)
  • Deodorizer service and replacement
  • Written log left in each restroom after service

Service Logs

Every restroom service leaves a dated and initialed log card on-site. This is a simple record — time in, time out, initials — that gives building managers and tenants visible confirmation that the service happened. For medical and dental buildings, the log is part of the documentation trail for infection control compliance.

Frequency Options

Most Saint Paul commercial buildings run restroom sanitation on the same schedule as nightly cleaning — five nights per week. High-traffic buildings in the Lowertown entertainment district, office lobbies with ground-floor retail, and multi-tenant buildings with shared restrooms may add midday service on peak-traffic days. We schedule based on your building's actual pattern, not a default.

Supply management: We track supply usage and flag reorder needs before you run out. Supply cost is billed separately at cost — we don't mark up consumables.
